WHAT IS SANS

Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome

Prolonged microgravity during long-duration spaceflight produces neuro-ophthalmic findings in astronauts that together define SANS. NASA physicians first described them in ISS astronauts in 2011 (Mader et al.). Clinical manifestations reviewed by Lee et al. (2020):

  • Unilateral and bilateral optic disc edema
  • Globe flattening
  • Choroidal and retinal folds
  • Hyperopic refractive error shifts
  • Focal ischemic retina (cotton wool spots)

The cause is not settled. Proposed mechanisms, terrestrial analogs and countermeasures (e.g. lower body negative pressure, nutritional supplementation, translaminar pressure modulation) are reviewed by Ong et al. (2023), with NASA co-authors.

NASA OCT EVIDENCE · ISS OCULAR HEALTH STUDY

OCT measures these changes in flight

STUDY
Macias et al., JAMA Ophthalmology 2020 — prospective cohort
CREW
11 astronauts, before, during and after ~6-month ISS missions (mean 170 days)
FINDING
Optic nerve head rim tissue and peripapillary choroid thickened from early flight, with the largest change near the end of the mission
MAGNITUDE
Mean rim tissue thickness change on flight day 150: +35.7 µm
Why it matters hereNASA tracks SANS with repeated OCT compared against each astronaut's own pre-flight baseline — the same longitudinal design this prototype implements.

NASA OPEN SCIENCE DATA REPOSITORY

OSD-816 — mechanism research

“The ex vivo human translaminar autonomous system to study spaceflight associated neuro-ocular syndrome pathogenesis (Retina, RT-PCR and electroretinography)”

MATERIAL
De-identified human donor eyes (retina), cultured ex vivo
FACTORS
Pressure ratio ICP:IOP (intracranial : intraocular) and optic nerve tilt
ASSAYS
Real-time PCR, electroretinography
TYPE
Ground study

Relevance: it studies the pressure-difference hypothesis for SANS in human retinal tissue. It is not astronaut clinical data and contains no OCT scans, so it is used here as scientific background, not as model training data.

PATH TO ASTRONAUT DATA

NASA Life Sciences Data Archive (LSDA)

Astronaut research data such as in-flight OCT are archived by NASA and are restricted. Access is through the NASA Life Sciences Portal: a formal data request with a Data Use Agreement, reviewed by a board before approval.

Next step for this project: request de-identified peripapillary OCT from the ISS Ocular Health study to validate the pipeline on astronauts and extend segmentation to the optic nerve head.
